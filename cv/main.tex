\section*{WORKING PAPERS}

    \noindent
    \textbf{The Impact of WWI on Migration Flows and Migrant Integration in France} \hfill \textbf{Job Market Paper} \\
    Abstract: With 1.4 million deaths and 1.1 million severely wounded soldiers, World War I cost France more than 10\% of its labor force. This paper investigates how migration acted as a buffer against the resulting localized imbalances in the labor and marriage markets. I construct a novel department-level panel covering 1900--1936, combining historical French census reports with micro-level genealogical records that track individual migration trajectories and integration. Using a difference-in-differences design with a continuous treatment, the military casualty rate of each department, I estimate the causal effect of missing men on the volume and composition of both internal and international migration flows. Beyond aggregate flows, I examine migrants' economic and social assimilation through sectoral labor market participation, intermarriage rates, and cultural integration proxied by children's naming patterns. \\

    \noindent
    \textbf{The Rise of International Migration: Evidence from Genealogical Data} \hfill Work in Progress, 2026 \\
    \textit{with Simone Moriconi and Riccardo Turati} \\
    Abstract: Research on the drivers of historical European migration has been constrained by the scarcity of longitudinal data. Unlike traditional census records, which provide cross-sectional snapshots, genealogical data enable the continuous tracking of individuals across their entire life cycles and geographical trajectories. Leveraging a novel genealogical dataset covering 1700--1940, which we validate against census records from the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, France, Norway, and Sweden, we estimate a gravity model to identify the determinants of the largest intra-European and transatlantic migration flows between Europe, the United States, and Canada. We measure the role of historical economic, political, and cultural distance between origins and destinations.
